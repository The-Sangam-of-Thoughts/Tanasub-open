# Layer 0 — Blog, Social, and Marketing Structure

Use this layer for blog posts, newsletters, landing pages, product descriptions, marketing emails, Instagram captions, LinkedIn posts, and X threads. Define the audience, desired response, platform, and factual burden before drafting. Load a genre or subgenre instruction only when the piece actually uses that form.

## Set the writing brief

Write one line for each:

| Decision | Required answer |
|---|---|
| Reader | Who will read this, what do they already know, and what language do they use? |
| Need | What problem, desire, or question brings them here? |
| Promise | What specific value will they receive? |
| Action | What should they understand, feel, or do afterward? |
| Register | Casual, professional, specialist, playful, urgent, or another named voice. |
| Constraints | Platform limit, length, format, brand rules, accessibility, and deadline. |

If a material brief field is unknown, state the assumption. Never invent audience research, product performance, testimonials, scarcity, or results.

## Choose a short structure

Use three to five functional units. Pick the smallest structure that carries the promise.

| Purpose | Structure |
|---|---|
| Explain or teach | Hook → problem/context → explanation → example → takeaway |
| Persuade | Hook → reader problem → supported benefit → proof/limits → action |
| Share a story | Moment of change → tension → turning point → meaning → reader connection |
| Announce | News first → why it matters → essential details → next step |
| Compare | Decision criterion → options → tradeoffs → recommendation → exception |

Start with what matters to the reader. Use one primary idea per section. Delete any introduction that merely announces the topic and any conclusion that only repeats it.

## Build each unit

For every section or paragraph:

1. State a concrete point.
2. Show it with an example, observation, fact, or useful detail.
3. Explain why it matters to this reader.
4. Give a next step when the purpose is practical.

Use a hook from [Layer 1](layer1-storytelling.md) when attention must be earned. A hook must lead honestly into the piece; it must not manufacture fear, certainty, urgency, or a promise the body cannot deliver.

## Match evidence to the claim

Blog and marketing work can use a lighter evidence burden than academic writing, but accuracy does not become optional.

- Cite or link consequential facts, statistics, quotations, health or safety statements, comparisons, and claims about people or products.
- Label personal experience, customer evidence, examples, estimates, and opinion as what they are.
- Treat a case study as one bounded example, not universal proof. Disclose material sponsorship or affiliation.
- Check names, dates, numbers, links, quotation wording, and the current platform or product facts before publication.
- A low-stakes creative line may rely on voice and observation. A claim that could affect money, health, reputation, rights, or a purchase needs direct, suitable evidence and its limits.

When evidence is missing, narrow or remove the claim. Do not fill the gap with confident language.

## Make it easy to scan

- Put the main value in the heading and opening lines.
- Keep paragraphs focused; vary their length instead of forcing every paragraph to match.
- Use descriptive subheadings for longer pieces. Use bullets only for genuinely parallel items.
- Front-load labels and key terms. Prefer specific verbs and concrete nouns.
- Make link text describe its destination. Do not rely on colour, emoji, or formatting alone to carry meaning.
- Use emphasis sparingly; a page of bold text has no emphasis.

## Adapt to the platform

| Format | Drafting constraint |
|---|---|
| Blog/newsletter | Give skimmers a clear heading path while keeping a coherent full read. |
| Instagram | Stay within 2,200 characters; place the reader promise before truncation and make hashtags optional metadata. |
| X post/thread | Keep each post within 280 characters; make every post intelligible while maintaining the thread's progression. |
| LinkedIn | Treat about 3,000 characters as a practical target, then verify the current limit; put the useful claim before the fold. |
| Marketing email | Align subject, preview, body promise, and one primary action. Avoid deceptive urgency. |
| Landing page | Match headline to audience need; connect each benefit to support; make the next action explicit. |
| Product description | Lead with use and fit, distinguish features from benefits, and state material limitations. |

Platform limits can change. Verify them when compliance matters.

## Draft handoff

Before enhancement, confirm that the draft has one reader, one promise, an appropriate three-to-five-unit structure, accurate claims, a visible takeaway or action, and no copied stock paragraph. Then apply [Layer 1](layer1-storytelling.md), [Layer 2](layer2-humanize.md), and finally [Layer 3](layer3-qa-gate.md).
