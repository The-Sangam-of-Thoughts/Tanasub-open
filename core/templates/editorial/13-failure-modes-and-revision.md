# Failure Modes and Revision: {{preferred_label}}

## Diagnostic method

For each finding, record the observable symptom, affected reader promise, likely cause, supporting claim and evidence IDs, diagnostic question, repair options, verification method, and risk of overcorrection.

## Structural failures

Cover causality, progression or functional equivalent, stakes, escalation, turning points, climax, resolution, continuity, and medium-specific structure.

## Character and relationship failures

Cover agency, motivation, role clarity, arcs or functional equivalents, opposition, ensemble balance, stereotype, and relationship development.

## Scene, unit, pacing, and emotion failures

Cover entry and exit, movement, information flow, tension, release, repetition, fatigue, surprise, and accessibility load across the relevant unit of composition.

## Voice, dialogue, language, and prose failures

Cover consistency, clarity, exposition, subtext, dialect, translation, style, devices, attribution, and source-language limits.

## Setting, research, and representation failures

Cover world consistency, factual accuracy, cultural specificity, Indian and regional representation, sensitivity, copyright, and unsupported claims.

## Screenwriting and adaptation failures

Where relevant, diagnose only writing-level issues and distinguish them from acting, direction, editing, sound, design, or production. Where irrelevant, document the functional equivalent.

## Revision sequence

Order repairs from reader promise and architecture through unit-level movement, language, fact/evidence verification, accessibility, sensitivity, citations, links, and final independent review.

## Final checks

Confirm all mandatory topics or functional equivalents, claim-to-evidence traceability, counterevidence, scope, exceptions, limitations, professional-versus-audience reception separation, cross-links, and no unverified quotations or translations.
