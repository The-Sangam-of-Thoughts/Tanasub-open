# Voice, Style, Dialogue, and Point of View: {{preferred_label}}

## Voice and style repertoire

Analyse diction, syntax, register, tone, rhythm, narrative distance, tense, texture, and category-specific language patterns. Tie recommendations to claim IDs and evidence IDs.

## Point of view and mediation

Explain viewpoint options, focalization, speaker, persona, narrator reliability, camera or script perspective, listener address, participant agency, and functional equivalents.

## Dialogue, subtext, silence, and exposition

Teach spoken exchange or its equivalent, including subtext, silence, dialect, exposition, captions, voice-over, chorus, interface text, or visual/aural writing cues as appropriate.

## Indian, regional, and multilingual practice

Address evidenced language traditions, code-switching, dialect, register, orality, and regional usage without exoticising or treating English as neutral. Require sensitivity and relevant language competence.

## Translation limitations

Name original language, edition, and translator where applicable. Distinguish effects verified in the source text from effects observed only in translation; do not attribute a translator's wording to the original author.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, distinguish script-level dialogue, scene description, voice-over, and structure from acting, direction, cinematography, sound design, and editing.

## Diagnostics, revision, and accessibility

Diagnose inconsistent voice, unintended opacity, exposition load, indistinguishable speakers, harmful dialect rendering, inaccessible formatting, and caption or transcript issues. Provide read-aloud, attribution, register, and clarity tests.

## Evidence and cross-links

Record counterevidence, scope, exceptions, confidence, and links to character, setting, devices, close readings, exercises, and revision.
