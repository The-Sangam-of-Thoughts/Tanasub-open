# History, Traditions, and Evolution: {{preferred_label}}

## Periodization method

Explain how periods were chosen, which source and work IDs support them, and where the periodization is disputed, region-limited, or a researcher inference.

## Origins and antecedents

Describe precursors without claiming identity before recognition evidence supports it. Distinguish retrospective classification from contemporary self-description.

## Formative stage

Identify movements, works, creators, institutions, technologies, and writing practices that shaped the axis. Connect every consequential account to claim IDs.

## Recognized and contemporary stages

Trace turning points, changes in reader contract, craft mechanics, circulation, and present variation. Avoid a single linear or Western-centred history.

## Indian, regional, and tradition histories

Address Indian languages and regions, oral, classical, devotional, folk, modernist, postcolonial, and other evidenced traditions as appropriate. Explain independent development, exchange, contested equivalence, and gaps in the record.

## Translation and transmission

Name original languages, translators or editions consulted, and how translation, adaptation, publication, or market categories shaped reception. State which language-level conclusions cannot be verified.

## Screenwriting and adaptation history

Where relevant, analyse changes in writing practice across prose, stage, screenplay, film, television, audio, or interactive media. Exclude production history unless it changes a writing-level claim. If irrelevant, record the relevance decision and functional equivalent.

## Counterhistories and limitations

Present contrary accounts, exclusions, archival silences, access limitations, confidence, and the scope of each conclusion.

## Craft consequences and cross-links

Translate historical findings into scoped practitioner implications and link to definition, conventions, close readings, reception analysis, and sources.
