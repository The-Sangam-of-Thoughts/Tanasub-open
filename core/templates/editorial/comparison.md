# Neighbour Comparison: {{preferred_label}}

## Comparison question and neighbour

Identify the nearest non-equivalent axis by stable ID, explain why it is the appropriate neighbour, and state the evidence scope. Link to both canonical packages and the genre map.

## Stable dimensions

Compare definition, recognition, reader promise, emotional engine, structure or progression, character functions or equivalents, setting assumptions, voice/style, conflict/stakes, pacing, conventions, climax/ending, historical tradition, audience, and medium. Explain inapplicable dimensions and compare their functional equivalents.

## Similarities without collapse

Describe shared features with supporting claim and evidence IDs. Do not turn overlap, influence, shared marketing, or hybridity into identity.

## Decisive boundaries

For each difference, state the writing-level consequence, representative work IDs, counterexamples, scope, exceptions, confidence, and disputed readings.

## Hybrids and transitions

Explain how works may combine or move between the axes while each canonical category remains distinct. Record multiple parentage without duplicating research files.

## Indian, regional, tradition, and translation scope

Test whether the comparison holds across evidenced Indian languages, regions, traditions, and translations. Do not make a global boundary from one market or translation.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, compare script-level or textual practices across media and distinguish them from performance or production. Otherwise record the relevance decision and medium-specific equivalent.

## Practitioner diagnostic

Provide questions that help a writer decide which promise and craft system the draft actually follows, plus revision options when signals conflict. Include accessibility and audience implications.

## Reception and cross-links

Keep professional criticism separate from audience response. Link to definitions, reader promise, conventions, close readings, reception analyses, claim matrices, and sources for both axes.
