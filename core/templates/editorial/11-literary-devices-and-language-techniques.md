# Literary Devices and Language Techniques: {{preferred_label}}

## Device-to-effect map

For each useful device, record its name, intended effect, evidence and claim IDs, best timing, execution method, scope, exceptions, and functional equivalent in other forms or media.

## Language and formal techniques

Analyse imagery, metaphor, symbolism, irony, pattern, repetition, contrast, sound, syntax, typography, fragmentation, silence, montage-as-written, interactivity, and other evidenced techniques.

## Culturally specific devices

Describe Indian, regional, oral, classical, devotional, folk, multilingual, or other tradition-specific techniques on their own terms, with appropriate source and language competence. Avoid flattening distinct practices into decorative equivalents.

## Translation limitations

Identify devices verified in the original language, altered or introduced by translation, and unavailable for direct verification. Name editions and translators in the supporting records.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, distinguish devices encoded in script, dialogue, scene order, or textual cues from effects created by acting, direction, cinematography, sound, or editing.

## Misuse, overuse, and diagnostics

Show observable failure patterns, diagnostic questions, revision methods, and risks of removing purposeful difficulty. Include accessibility effects of typography, formatting, sensory density, and navigation.

## Evidence and cross-links

Record counterevidence, disagreement, confidence, limitations, and links to voice, setting, theme, conventions, close readings, exercises, and revision.
