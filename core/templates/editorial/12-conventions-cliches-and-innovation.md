# Conventions, Cliches, and Innovation: {{preferred_label}}

## Convention inventory

Classify conventions as required, common but optional, historical, regional, market-specific, disputed, or obsolete. Cite claim and evidence IDs for every consequential classification.

## Reader-contract function

Explain what each convention signals or accomplishes and how it interacts with reader promise, emotional engine, structure, character or functional equivalent, and ending.

## Cliches and exhausted execution

Distinguish a shared convention from an exhausted expression. Diagnose predictability, stereotype, cultural appropriation, mechanical imitation, and innovation that merely obscures the contract.

## Subversion and genre blending

Show how to retain, invert, combine, or reject conventions intentionally. State the consequences, required setup, neighbouring axes, hybrid relationships, and evidence limits.

## Indian, regional, tradition, and translation scope

Do not label unfamiliar cultural practices as clichés. Analyse conventions within their own language, period, tradition, and audience contexts and record translation-dependent uncertainty.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, identify conventions encoded in writing and how adaptation alters them. Keep production and performance conventions separate unless they change a writing-level claim.

## Innovation diagnostics and revision

Test whether an innovation is legible, purposeful, accessible, and consistent; whether it preserves or knowingly renegotiates the reader contract; and whether unintended harm or flattening remains.

## Evidence and cross-links

Record counterevidence, scope, exceptions, confidence, professional criticism, audience response, and links to definition, history, devices, comparison, reception, and revision.
