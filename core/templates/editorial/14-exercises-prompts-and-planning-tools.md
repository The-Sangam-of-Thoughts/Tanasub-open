# Exercises, Prompts, and Planning Tools: {{preferred_label}}

## Exercise contract

For every exercise state: skill trained; linked claim and evidence IDs; difficulty; form or medium; cultural and language scope; materials; constraints; steps; completion evidence; self-diagnostic; revision move; accessibility alternative; and cross-links to guidance and failure modes.

## Foundation exercises

Create short drills for reader promise, emotional engine, premise, theme, title, opening, voice, setting, and the smallest meaningful compositional unit.

## Imitation and transformation exercises

Use only brief, copyright-compliant references to verified primary works. Require analysis of technique rather than copying protected expression. Include translation-aware options and distinguish source-language from translated effects.

## Structure and scene or unit drills

Practise progression, causality or functional equivalent, character/relationship movement, tension, pacing, climax or culmination, and ending.

## Indian, regional, and tradition-aware exercises

Ground exercises in evidenced traditions and appropriate language/context. Do not ask writers to imitate a culture they have not researched; offer research, sensitivity, and positionality steps.

## Screenwriting and adaptation exercises

Where relevant, practise script-level beats, scenes, dialogue, adaptation decisions, and reception goals while excluding production-only effects. Where irrelevant, provide the axis-specific medium equivalent.

## Progressive rewriting sequence

Include diagnosis-led rewrites for structural, unit-level, language, representation, and accessibility issues, increasing in complexity without changing the skill target.

## Complete project prompt and planning worksheet

Provide a start-to-finish brief covering promise, audience, premise, structure, roles or equivalents, setting, voice, pacing, devices, conventions, research, evidence, cultural scope, revision, accessibility, and independent review.

## Cross-links and validation

Link each exercise to at least one analysis file and one failure diagnostic. State how a reviewer can verify that the exercise tests the intended skill rather than generic effort.
