# Setting, Atmosphere, and Worldbuilding: {{preferred_label}}

## Place, time, and social world

Define the physical, temporal, institutional, ecological, technological, and social dimensions that matter to the axis. Connect claims to evidence and work IDs.

## Atmosphere and sensory design

Explain how language, image, sound-as-written, rhythm, selection, omission, and viewpoint create mood. Where setting is abstract or minimal, state the functional equivalent.

## World rules and consistency

Record explicit and implicit rules, constraints, costs, knowledge boundaries, continuity requirements, and permissible ambiguity.

## Research and representation

Specify the factual, cultural, historical, geographical, scientific, or professional research required. Distinguish verified fact from invention and researcher inference.

## Indian, regional, tradition, and translation scope

Represent places, languages, communities, oral or written traditions, and regional histories at appropriate specificity. Flag translation effects, outsider limits, sensitivity needs, and evidence gaps; never use one Indian setting as representative of all India.

## Screen, stage, audio, and interactive adaptation

Where relevant, analyse what the script or text establishes and distinguish it from design, cinematography, performance, sound production, or implementation.

## Diagnostics, revision, and accessibility

Diagnose generic setting, exposition dump, broken rules, sensory monotony, cultural flattening, spatial confusion, and accessibility barriers. Provide continuity, orientation, sensory-balance, and sensitivity checks.

## Evidence and cross-links

Record claim IDs, support, counterevidence, scope, exceptions, confidence, limitations, and links to history, structure, voice, atmosphere/pacing, close readings, and revision.
