# Titles, Openings, Climaxes, and Endings: {{preferred_label}}

## Prompt and title strategy

Explain how to interpret a brief or prompt, develop title candidates, test genre signals, avoid misleading promises, and account for language or market variation. Cite claim IDs.

## First line or opening unit

Analyse first lines, images, beats, scenes, stanzas, sequences, invocations, interface states, or other functional equivalents. State what the opening promises and withholds.

## Opening situation

Teach orientation, desire, instability, voice, setting, stakes, and momentum at an axis-appropriate scale. Include accessibility and content-warning implications.

## Climactic obligation or functional equivalent

Define the change, confrontation, recognition, convergence, intensification, return, or formal culmination the work prepares. Avoid imposing a conventional climax where evidence does not support one.

## Ending and aftermath

Compare closure, ambiguity, denouement, coda, recurrence, rupture, sequel hooks, and reader aftereffect. Identify which options fulfil or deliberately renegotiate the reader contract.

## Indian, regional, tradition, and translation variation

Analyse evidenced naming, opening, and closure practices without presuming Western narrative norms. Note source-language wordplay, translated titles, edition variance, and limitations.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, distinguish scripted opening/climax/ending choices from performance, direction, editing, music, and distribution decisions.

## Diagnostics and revision

Diagnose generic titles, delayed entry, broken opening promise, unearned climax, false ending, over-explanation, inaccessible framing, and mismatched hooks. Provide tests and repairs.

## Evidence and cross-links

Record support, counterevidence, scope, exceptions, confidence, and links to premise, structure, emotion/pacing, conventions, exercises, close readings, and revision.
