# Character, Relationships, and Arcs: {{preferred_label}}

## Character system

Identify roles, desires, agency, opposition, vulnerabilities, resources, and ensemble functions. Distinguish recurrent patterns from mandatory archetypes.

## Relationships and pressure

Show how bonds, conflicts, obligations, power, intimacy, community, and institutions generate movement. Cite claim and evidence IDs.

## Arcs and transformations

Analyse change, refusal, revelation, disintegration, cyclical development, static pressure, or collective transformation. When conventional characters or arcs do not apply, explain why and teach the functional equivalent, such as speaker, persona, chorus, image-system, voice, or participant role.

## Dialogue and non-verbal relation

Connect relationship mechanics to speech, subtext, silence, gesture-as-written, narration, interface choice, or other textual signals.

## Cultural, Indian, regional, and translation scope

Examine kinship, social role, language, dialect, caste, class, gender, community, and tradition only where evidence supports the analysis. Flag translation-dependent characterization and sensitivity risks.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, separate characterization supplied by text or script from performance, casting, direction, and production. Record changes caused by adaptation at the writing level.

## Diagnostics and revision

Diagnose weak agency, interchangeable roles, unearned change, stereotype, relationship discontinuity, and inaccessible attribution. Provide targeted repair tests.

## Evidence and cross-links

Record counterevidence, scope, exceptions, confidence, limitations, and links to structure, voice/dialogue, emotion, close readings, exercises, and revision.
