# Definition, Boundaries, and Subgenres: {{preferred_label}}

## Definition and recognition

State the working definition, recognition threshold used, corpus warrant, distinct writing practices, and supporting source IDs. Identify whether evidence is source-stated, synthesis, or researcher inference.

## Axis type and distinctiveness

Classify the axis as form, genre, subgenre, mode, structure, audience, medium, tradition, or hybrid. Evaluate reader promise, emotional engine, plot or structural logic, character functions, setting assumptions, voice, conflict, and ending conventions. Cite claim IDs.

## Names and disputes

Record the preferred label, aliases, historical, regional, language-specific, and marketing names; contested definitions; and disagreements. Explain which labels are true synonyms and which are related but non-equivalent.

## Boundaries

Define broader, narrower, parent, child, related, and hybrid relationships. For each neighbour, state the shared features and the decisive boundary. Link to the genre map and comparison analysis.

## Inclusions and exclusions

Give evidence-backed inclusion and exclusion tests with representative work IDs. Do not infer a universal boundary from one market or language.

## Indian, regional, and translation considerations

Record Indian and regional terminology and traditions without forcing equivalence. Identify original languages, editions or translations consulted, translator where applicable, and limits on claims about language-specific effects. If evidence is insufficient, record the search evidence and scope limitation.

## Screen and adaptation relevance

State whether screenplay, stage, film, television, audio, oral, or interactive use changes the boundary. Separate writing-level classification evidence from production or performance evidence.

## Functional-equivalent check

For every standard boundary dimension that does not apply, explain why and specify the axis-specific function used instead; no section may be left empty.

## Evidence and cross-links

List claim IDs, supporting and contrary source/work IDs, scope, exceptions, confidence, and links to history, reader-promise, comparison, genre-map, and sources files.
