# How to Write {{preferred_label}}

## Purpose and evidence contract

Write a self-contained practitioner guide for this axis. Every consequential recommendation must cite one or more claim IDs and its supporting source or work IDs. State counterevidence, scope, exceptions, confidence, and evidence limitations where they affect the advice. Distinguish quotation, paraphrase, synthesis, and researcher inference. Do not introduce claims absent from the approved claim matrix.

## What the category is

Define the axis, its reader-facing identity, boundaries, aliases, neighbours, hybrids, and exclusions. Link to `01-definition-boundaries-and-subgenres.md`. If the axis is a form, mode, structure, audience, medium, tradition, or hybrid, describe it accurately rather than calling it a genre.

## Reader promise and emotional engine

Explain expected experiences, emotional sequence, pleasures, discomforts, surprises, and forms of satisfaction. Identify differences across audiences, regions, languages, traditions, and media. Cite claim IDs and link to `03-reader-promise-and-emotional-engine.md`.

## Develop the idea

Give an evidence-led method for generating and testing premises, thematic tensions, moral questions, and meanings. Connect advice to `04-premise-theme-and-meaning.md` and record where counterexamples limit a general rule.

## Plan the work

Provide a practical planning sequence for structure, causality, escalation, character or functional equivalent, relationships, setting, world assumptions, and research. Link to files `05` through `08`.

## Title and opening

Explain prompt interpretation, title choices, first lines or opening beats, initial situations, and the promise established at the beginning. For screen, stage, audio, oral, or interactive work, explain the medium-specific opening unit.

## Voice, point of view, and dialogue

Cover diction, syntax, tone, narrative distance, viewpoint, tense, dialogue, subtext, silence, dialect, exposition, and their functional equivalents. Treat language and dialect responsibly; identify translation-dependent observations and what cannot be inferred from a translation alone.

## Conflict, stakes, pacing, and emotion

Explain pressure, anticipation, withholding, release, scene or unit rhythm, surprise, revelation, and fatigue. If conventional conflict, suspense, scenes, or plot do not apply, name the reason and teach the axis-specific mechanism serving the same function.

## Climax and resolution

Describe climactic obligations, culmination or functional equivalent, denouement, closure, ambiguity, recurrence, and sequel or continuation hooks where applicable.

## Devices, conventions, and innovation

Explain the techniques that produce category-specific effects, including culturally or linguistically specific devices. Separate required from optional conventions, diagnose clichés, and show how innovation can preserve or deliberately renegotiate the reader contract.

## Indian, regional, and tradition contexts

Integrate verified Indian works, languages, regions, oral or written traditions, and critical contexts wherever evidence exists. Do not treat India as one tradition or assume equivalence with a non-Indian category. When evidence is insufficient, document the searches and limitation instead of asserting absence.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Where relevant, distinguish writing-level choices in screenplay, stage, film, television, audio, or interactive adaptations from performance, direction, or production choices. Where not relevant, record the relevance decision and provide the medium-specific equivalent.

## Diagnose and revise

Map observable symptoms to likely causes, diagnostic questions, and structural, scene/unit, prose/language, sensitivity, and accessibility repairs. Link to `13-failure-modes-and-revision.md`.

## Start-to-finish workflow

Present an ordered workflow from idea and evidence check through planning, drafting, revision, accessibility review, citation verification, and independent editorial review. Include stop conditions for unsupported claims, unverified quotations or translations, and copyright risk.

## Practice

Select progressive exercises from `14-exercises-prompts-and-planning-tools.md`; state the skill trained, evidence or claim IDs, constraints, completion evidence, and self-diagnostic.

## Sources, limitations, and cross-links

Link to `15-sources-and-further-reading.md`, the package claim matrix, primary-work records, three close readings, neighbour comparison, reception analysis, genre map, Indian-literature atlas, and screenwriting/reception materials where relevant. Separate professional criticism from audience response and state unresolved disagreement.

## Section-purpose matrix and handoff

Before delivery, add a compact author-only matrix outside the publishable guide or in the associated handoff record: heading; distinct reader purpose; claim/source/work IDs; concrete example or observation; form-specific functional equivalent; diagnostic output; revision action; and limitation. A narrative term such as scene, character, plot, climax, or dialogue may be replaced only when the equivalent preserves the instructional function and the reason is stated.

Return the guide path, approved template and claim-matrix revisions, all changed paths, finding/claim mapping, unresolved evidence or rights limits, and author-check results. Do not mark this guide reviewed, approved, publishable, or complete on the strength of author checks or automated output.
