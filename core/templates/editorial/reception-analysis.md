# Reception and Failure-Pattern Analysis: {{preferred_label}}

## Question, corpus, and limits

Define the reception question, period, languages, regions, media, works, platforms or publications, sampling method, access conditions, and representativeness limits. Use at least three independent professional reviews for professional-reception conclusions.

## Professional criticism

Analyse positive, mixed, and negative professional criticism separately. Record source IDs, locators, dates, independence, claims, counterevidence, scope, exceptions, and confidence.

## Audience response

Analyse audience or reader response in its own section. State sampling and platform biases and never use popularity, ratings, or comments as craft authority, proof of intent, or a substitute for professional criticism.

## Writing-level failure patterns

Map recurring responses to textual symptoms, plausible craft causes, competing explanations, and evidence. Distinguish writing from marketing, distribution, performance, direction, editing, design, music, and other production factors.

## Success patterns and disagreement

Record which writing choices were praised, by whom, under what conditions, and where reviewers or audiences disagreed. Avoid averaging away culturally meaningful disagreement.

## Indian, regional, tradition, and translation reception

Include relevant Indian and regional critical contexts, language communities, editions, translators, and circulation conditions. State when reception concerns a translation or adaptation rather than the source-language work.

## Screenwriting and adaptation reception

Where relevant, isolate script-level evidence and compare source and adaptation promises. Where no writing-level inference is possible, say so and report only the supported reception finding.

## Practitioner diagnostics and revision

Convert supported failure patterns into diagnostic questions and conditional revision options. Include accessibility, content-warning, and sensitivity considerations and the risk of overfitting to one audience.

## Evidence and cross-links

Link every consequential conclusion to claim IDs and source/work IDs, and cross-link to reader promise, pacing, conventions, failure/revision, close readings, comparison, and sources.

## Review-sample register

List each professional review separately with source ID, publication, reviewer, publication date, exact locator, independence group, stance, writing-level observation, and verification-event ID. Three reviews that repeat, syndicate, share an author, or derive from the same originating assessment do not satisfy the independent-review floor. Record audience material separately with platform, sampling method, bias, and the narrow reception conclusion it supports.
