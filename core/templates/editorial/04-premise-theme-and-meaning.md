# Premise, Theme, and Meaning: {{preferred_label}}

## Suitable premise patterns

Describe category-specific premise components, generative tensions, scales, and constraints. Mark each consequential recommendation with claim IDs and supporting work/source IDs.

## Theme and moral questions

Map recurring thematic tensions and ethical questions without treating recurrence as obligation. Distinguish evidence-backed patterns from researcher interpretation.

## Symbolism and meaning-making

Explain useful symbolic, structural, linguistic, visual, aural, or performative devices and their intended effects. Where symbolism is not the operative mechanism, teach its functional equivalent.

## Cultural and historical scope

Compare Indian, regional, language, tradition, and period contexts. Avoid extracting a supposedly universal meaning from one cultural corpus.

## Translation, screenwriting, and adaptation

Identify meanings dependent on source language or form. Where relevant, explain how adaptation changes premise or thematic delivery at the writing level; separate these choices from production execution.

## Thematic failure diagnostics

Diagnose didacticism, incoherence, accidental implication, cliché, cultural flattening, and unsupported allegory. Provide revision tests and accessibility or sensitivity checks.

## Counterevidence and limitations

Record contrary works or scholarship, scope, exceptions, confidence, and unresolved questions.

## Cross-links

Link to reader promise, structure, devices, conventions, close readings, exercises, revision, and sources.
