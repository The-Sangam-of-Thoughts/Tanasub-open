# Research Report Style Guide

**Scope:** S03 templates and every later evidence-led library report  
**Canonical format:** Markdown  
**Authority:** The approved master plan, S01 schemas, source policy, and quality rubric take precedence

## Reader and purpose

Write for working writers who need usable decisions, diagnostics, and revision moves. A report must teach how a category works rather than merely define or celebrate it. State the reader promise, show the craft mechanism, explain its likely effect, name its limits, and turn it into an observable writing or revision action.

## Evidence voice

- Separate verified source statements, paraphrases, synthesis across sources, and researcher inference.
- Attach consequential craft advice to claim IDs and resolvable source or primary-work IDs.
- State confidence, scope, exceptions, counterevidence, and evidence limitations near the claim they qualify.
- Never convert popularity, prizes, sales, or one famous example into proof of quality or universality.
- Never invent quotations, translations, bibliographic facts, examples presented as real, or research conclusions.
- Use audience response only for audience-reception claims; keep it distinct from professional criticism.

## Structure and navigation

Begin with the practical promise and boundaries. Use descriptive sentence-case headings, short paragraphs, tables only when comparison benefits, and lists for genuinely parallel items. Each standalone file must orient the reader, link to its evidence and related analyses, and end with diagnostics, revision actions, limitations, and next links when those functions apply.

Required cross-links use relative repository paths. Citations use stable record identifiers such as `[@src-example]`, `[@work-example]`, and `[@clm-example]` only when matching records exist. Do not place raw URLs where a durable source record should carry access metadata.

## Terminology

Use the canonical axis types: form, genre, subgenre, mode, structure, audience, medium, tradition, and hybrid. Do not call a mode, audience, structure, medium, or marketing label a genre. Use the preferred label in prose and preserve aliases, regional names, contested definitions, and historical terms in the boundary record.

Use `primary work` for the creative object being analysed, `source` for evidence about a work or craft question, `professional reception` for criticism or long-form review, and `audience reception` for reader/viewer response. Use `Indian` only when the evidence supports that scope; name language, region, tradition, community, and translation route whenever they matter.

## Cultural and translation practice

- Do not treat Anglophone or Western convention as the unmarked default.
- Integrate Indian and regional evidence in the relevant craft section and cross-link dedicated coverage.
- If no direct Indian equivalent is found, document the search and nearest relevant traditions without forcing equivalence.
- Identify the language and edition actually consulted, the translator when applicable, and limits introduced by translation or unavailable originals.
- Avoid extracting culture-specific practice as universal technique without stated scope.

## Screenwriting and adaptation

Include film or television only for writing-level evidence: story conception, screenplay structure, character construction, dialogue, subtext, adaptation, genre development, cultural specificity, emotional design, suspense, revelation, endings, and reception of writing. Exclude acting, direction, editing, music, and cinematography unless they materially affect how screenplay-level writing is perceived, and label that inference.

## Examples and copyright

Prefer concise paraphrase and analytic description. Quote only when necessary, directly verified, accurately transcribed, attributed, located, and legally defensible. Do not store copyrighted books, scripts, screenplays, or substantial excerpts. Mark synthetic test content unmistakably and never cite it as research evidence.

## Practitioner components

Every actionable section should provide some combination of:

- an observable craft mechanism;
- a diagnostic question;
- a failure signal;
- a bounded revision move;
- an exercise tied to the analysis;
- a note on when the advice does not apply.

Do not prescribe one formula when evidence shows multiple valid practices. Present convention, alternatives, and deliberate subversion together.

## Accessibility and mechanics

Use plain language before specialist terminology, then define the specialist term. Keep heading order logical, tables readable without colour, links descriptive, abbreviations expanded on first use, and warnings specific. Avoid unexplained metaphor in instructions. Use Unicode only where it carries meaning; filenames and stable IDs remain lowercase ASCII kebab-case.

## Template tokens

Double-brace tokens are structural inputs, not publishable prose. Only names in `tools/qa/qa-config.json` are approved, and only inside configured template paths. Authors must resolve every token before a package can pass publication validation. Unknown tokens, approved tokens copied into package content, and ordinary markers such as unfinished-work abbreviations are blocking findings.

## Editorial finish

A finished report is internally consistent, traceable, culturally scoped, accessible, and useful without surrounding files. It acknowledges uncertainty and disagreement, contains no unresolved drafting marker, and does not claim that a taxonomy, dossier, package, family, or release is complete unless the corresponding gate evidence says so.

## Section-purpose and anti-padding rule

Every section begins with a one-sentence purpose that is distinct from adjacent sections and states its supporting claim or evidence IDs. A usable instructional passage follows this sequence where applicable: mechanism; a concrete, attributable example or observation; likely effect; diagnostic question or failure signal; bounded revision action; and scope or exception. A section may omit a narrative term only after naming the form-specific functional equivalent.

Do not meet a length target by restating definitions, swapping labels in stock paragraphs, repeating examples, or expanding generic encouragement. Each repeated recommendation must add a different supported condition, consequence, exception, or action. Editors reject copied or near-identical prose even if the package meets a word, heading, or file-count threshold.

Before handoff, authors provide a section-purpose matrix listing heading, purpose, claim/evidence IDs, form-specific equivalent where needed, diagnostic output, and revision action. A missing matrix entry makes that section incomplete; the matrix is an authoring aid, not evidence or approval.
