# Quality Rubric

**Use:** Independent editorial assessment of methods, templates, and later axis packages  
**Scale:** 0 absent; 1 unsafe or materially incomplete; 2 present but weak; 3 release-ready  
**Passing rule:** every blocking dimension scores 3; all other dimensions score at least 2; no open Critical, High, or evidence-affecting Medium finding

## Scoring dimensions

| Dimension | Blocking | Score 3 — release-ready | Score 2 — needs improvement | Score 1 or 0 — blocking condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teachability | yes | Explains mechanism, effect, diagnosis, and revision action for a working writer | Useful explanation lacks one practitioner component | Merely defines, lists, or prescribes without usable craft reasoning |
| Evidence fidelity | yes | Consequential claims resolve to evidence; expression type, verification, confidence, and limitations are explicit | Traceable but locally imprecise | Unverified fact, invented material, unresolved citation, or evidence/interpretation confusion |
| Disagreement and scope | yes | Counterevidence, exceptions, applicability, and contested positions are presented honestly | Limits are present but shallow | Advice is falsely universal or disagreement is concealed |
| Cultural and historical scope | yes | Multiple relevant periods/traditions are represented without treating one norm as universal | Coverage exists with a documented gap | Tokenism, forced equivalence, or unsupported generalisation |
| Indian and regional responsibility | yes when applicable | Indian-language, regional, oral, and translation evidence is integrated, or absence has a documented search and defensible explanation | Relevant coverage is thin but accurately limited | Hindi-only default, invented equivalence, or unsupported claim of absence |
| Translation integrity | yes when applicable | Consulted language/edition/translator and interpretive limitations are identified | Translation route is known but limitations need detail | Translation-dependent conclusion is presented as direct textual certainty |
| Primary-work mechanics | yes for work analysis | Analysis covers the required mechanics and explains how textual/screenwriting choices function | Mostly complete with bounded gaps | Plot summary substitutes for mechanics or completed fields are empty |
| Reception separation | yes when used | Professional and audience reception are separately sourced; contrasting views and underlying work are considered | Sources are adequate but coding is shallow | Popularity or audience response is treated as critical proof |
| Originality and convention | no | Convention, cliché, innovation, and deliberate subversion are distinguished | Conventional advice lacks nuance | Formula is enforced or novelty is asserted without comparison |
| Diagnostics and revision | no | Concrete failure signals, diagnostic questions, and bounded repairs follow from evidence | Some actionable help is present | Generic encouragement or unsupported rules replace diagnosis |
| Exercise alignment | no | Exercises practise a named mechanism and include success criteria and reflection | Exercise is relevant but weakly assessed | Prompt is decorative or unrelated to analysis |
| Accessibility | no | Plain language, logical headings, descriptive links, defined terms, and non-visual meaning | Minor clarity or navigation friction | Structure prevents independent use or relies on unexplained jargon |
| Cross-links and identity | yes | Canonical IDs, paths, relationships, citations, and related files resolve consistently | A non-blocking navigation improvement remains | Broken identity, orphan content, duplicate canonical location, or unresolved link |
| Copyright and privacy | yes | Metadata and brief defensible quotation only; rights/access limits are recorded; no secret or personal data leak | Attribution wording needs polish | Full copyrighted text, credential, secret, or unsafe personal data is included |
| Completion honesty | yes | Status matches evidence and required artifacts; limitations and open work are visible | A low-risk status clarification remains | Unresolved drafting marker, fabricated completion, self-approval, or gate bypass |

## Decision procedure

1. Validate structure and publication interlocks before editorial scoring.
2. Review 100% of quotations, translations, dates, identities, recognition decisions, numbers, disputed claims, limited-confidence claims, absence claims, and copyright restrictions.
3. Sample other consequential claims at 10% per batch, minimum five and maximum twenty; review all when fewer than five exist.
4. Include at least one Indian or regional record in each applicable batch.
5. Record evidence paths, scores, findings, owners, due dates, remediation, and independent retest.
6. A Critical or High finding blocks immediately. A Medium finding blocks when it affects evidence, classification, discoverability, accessibility, or required coverage.
7. A lane author may self-check but cannot issue the independent decision. A human gate cannot be inferred from an automated or agent review.

## Outcome labels

- `pass`: every blocking dimension is release-ready, other dimensions meet the floor, and required sign-offs exist.
- `ready-for-review`: author checks pass but independent or human review is pending.
- `fail`: at least one blocking dimension fails or required evidence is absent.
- `evidence-incomplete`: credible evidence cannot meet the required floor without padding; dependent drafting and release remain blocked.

Scores support judgment but never average away a blocking defect.

## Observable scoring anchors and failure examples

Score a blocking dimension `3` only when the reviewer can cite a concrete inspected example, its evidence path, and the applicable limitation. Score `2` when the required function is present but one recorded example, locator, scope boundary, or revision path needs repair. Score `1` when an assertion, example, or recommendation lacks its required evidence or usable mechanism. Score `0` when the function is absent, fabricated, or contradicted by the artifact.

Examples of automatic blocking failures include: a close reading assigned to a work that lacks category evidence; a quotation whose edition or locator was not checked; a general craft rule relabelled as axis evidence; three dependent reviews presented as independent reception; an unsupported regional absence claim; stock prose repeated to satisfy a section requirement; or a self-authored review presented as independent. These examples guide finding classification and do not substitute for inspection of the actual evidence.

For every score, record artifact path and heading, requirement ID, inspected record IDs and locators, observed result, finding ID where applicable, and reviewer identity. A score without this evidence trail is `unverified`, not a passing score. Critical failures and High failures always block; evidence-affecting Medium failures block regardless of other scores.
