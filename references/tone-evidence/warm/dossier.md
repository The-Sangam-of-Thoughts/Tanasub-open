# Warm — Research Dossier

- Tone ID: `tone-warm`
- Family: Elevation
- Researched: 2026-09-24
- Evidence status: complete

Scope of this tone: the narrator's voice carries **affiliative kindness toward the
reader** — the reader is addressed as a welcome, equal companion rather than a
subject to be informed or moved. Stakes still belong to the genre engine; the warm
voice owns texture. Nearest neighbors: `tone-compassionate` (care expressed *inside
another's suffering*) and `tone-nostalgic` (past-facing longing). Warmth is
present-facing and general; it is not consolation and not memory. It is also not
`tone-intimate`: the warm narrator is friendly without disclosing private
interiority. Genre engines own the target feeling; the warm voice must not
manufacture urgency or replace the promised emotional engine.

## B. Cognitive and psychological foundations

**Primacy of warmth (Asch 1946; Fiske, Cuddy & Glick 2007; Eisenbruch & Krasnow
2022).** Asch's classic impression-formation experiments showed that swapping the
single trait "warm" for "cold" in an otherwise identical trait list transformed
judges' whole impressions — warmth behaved as a *central* trait, changing the
meaning of the others, and the effect persisted when it was omitted. Fiske and
colleagues generalised this into the Stereotype Content Model: social perception
resolves into **warmth** (perceived intent — friend or foe) and **competence**
(perceived ability to enact that intent), and warmth is routinely judged first
because intent predicts behaviour. Eisenbruch and Krasnow (2022) offer an
evolutionary account of why warmth is prioritised, grounded in cooperative partner
choice. Craft consequence: a warm opening buys the reader's attention and colours
how every subsequent claim is read — which is why warmth is worth establishing
early, and why a warm surface with no substance is dangerous (`clm-tone-warm-warmth-primacy`).

**Warmth and competence as an interacting pair (Fiske et al. 2002).** The SCM's
central finding is that the dimensions are not additive: mixed combinations produce
*predictable, ambivalent* emotions. High warmth with low competence draws pity or
sympathy; low warmth with high competence draws envy; only high warmth *and* high
competence draws admiration and trust. This is the single most important craft
constraint on the tone: a warm voice that cannot deliver substance invites
condescension, not affection (`clm-tone-warm-warmth-without-substance`).

**Belongingness (Baumeister & Leary 1995).** The need to form and maintain
frequent, non-aversive relationships within an ongoing bond is described as a
powerful, pervasive motivation with wide effects on emotion and cognition. A warm
narrator simulates exactly this: a running, low-threat relationship in which the
reader is a member rather than a target (`clm-tone-warm-affiliation-belonging`).

**Parasocial interaction and direct address (Horton & Wohl 1956; Hartmann &
Goldhoorn 2011).** Horton and Wohl described mass media's "illusion of
face-to-face relationship" — intimacy at a distance. Hartmann and Goldhoorn's
2 × 2 experiment (*N* = 198) found that viewers reported more intense parasocial
experience when a performer addressed them on **both a bodily and a verbal level**;
perspective-taking ability and perceived attractiveness strengthened it further.
Craft consequence: explicit second-person address, questions, and conversational
turn-taking are not decoration — they are the mechanism by which a written voice
becomes a companion (`clm-tone-warm-parasocial-address`).

**Positive politeness (Brown & Levinson 1987).** Brown and Levinson's model
distinguishes positive politeness — approach-based redress oriented to the
addressee's positive face — from negative politeness (avoidance-based). Their
catalogue of positive-politeness strategies is effectively a checklist for warm
prose: notice and attend to the addressee, intensify interest, use in-group
identity markers, seek agreement, avoid disagreement, presuppose common ground,
joke, include both speaker and addressee in the activity, and assume reciprocity.
Leech (2014) adds the caution: on a sociopragmatic scale, **overpoliteness** can
register as sarcasm, and polite forms lose force when they become obligatory
(`clm-tone-warm-positive-politeness`; counterevidence for over-use).

**Convergence and over-accommodation (Giles et al. 1973; Dragojevic, Gasiorek &
Giles 2016).** Communication Accommodation Theory explains how speakers adjust
style toward (convergence) or away from (divergence) their audience; convergence
increases attraction, liking and relational solidarity. The same literature
documents **over-accommodation** — adapting beyond the optimal level — which is
perceived as condescending or patronizing (the "elderspeak" case). Warmth must be
calibrated *down* to the audience, not simplified *below* it
(`clm-tone-warm-convergence-overaccommodation`).

**Honest null.** No experimental study was found that directly isolates a "warm
literary narrator" as a stimulus and measures reader response. The mechanisms above
are supported by adjacent, well-replicated findings (impression formation,
belongingness, parasocial interaction, politeness), and the dossier keeps
consequential claims scoped to those findings rather than to an untested
"warm-voice effect."

## C. Rhetorical and literary tradition

**The familiar essay and the familiar letter.** Montaigne's *Essais* (1580) open
with "To the Reader," a direct address that frames the book as an informal,
unpolished, private communication between equals; the essays are deliberately
"conversational" and mix learned vocabulary with popular sayings and local slang.
Cicero's *Epistulae ad Familiares* (62–43 BCE, published posthumously by Tiro)
established the familiar letter as a genre of plain, affectionate, direct address
among friends and family. Together these are the historical engine of the warm
narrator: a speaker who writes *to* someone, at equal standing, without ceremony
(`clm-tone-warm-companionable-essay-letter`).

**The plain/conversational strain.** The warmth of the familiar mode has always
been a deliberate *choice against* ornament — the classical and later plain style
kept the reader alongside the speaker rather than beneath him. What changed: the
familiar mode migrated from salons and correspondence into the periodical column,
the how-to book, and the newsletter, where the conversational "you" became a
professional voice as much as a personal one. What remains contested: whether the
familiar voice *serves* the reader or *manages* them (the "chumminess" critique in
§G).

**The modern companionable craft voice (Lamott 1994).** Anne Lamott's *Bird by
Bird* is a sustained contemporary demonstration of the tone in instructional prose:
self-deprecating anecdotes, direct second-person address, humour that positions the
reader as a fellow struggler rather than a student, and a refusal of guru distance.
It is used here as a practitioner exemplar (Tier 4), not as a psychological
authority.

**Indian conversational prose.** Ghalib's Urdu letters (19th c.) are credited with
replacing the ornamental *mursala* convention with a conversational register — he
wrote as if speaking, sometimes beginning with only the addressee's name, and said
he wanted to write lines a reader would *enjoy*. Narayan's Malgudi stories carry a
gentle, ironic, companionable third-person voice that critics describe as warm,
humane and unpreachy. These are load-bearing for §E, not decorative.

## E. Cross-cultural survey

Translation limits are noted explicitly; no technique is asserted as a universal.

**Indian / Urdu–Tamil (load-bearing).** Three named strands. (i) **sakhya-rasa /
visrambha** — in Rūpa Gosvāmī's *Bhaktirasāmṛtasindhu*, the "mellow of friendship"
is defined as affectionate exchange between **equals**, "devoid of reverence and
deference but full of love, trust and confidence." This is a fully theorised
non-Anglophone account of affiliative warmth as an aesthetic state, and it connects
to the repository's shared claims `clm-rasa-emotional-aesthetic` and
`clm-dhvani-suggestion-theory` (warmth may be *suggested* rather than stated).
(ii) **Ghalib's letters** — conversational, humorous, informal Urdu prose that
deliberately made letter-writing "talk"; a foundational shift in Urdu prose toward
accessibility. (iii) **R.K. Narayan** — Malgudi stories whose narrators extend
gentle, ironic, non-judgemental companionship to ordinary people. Translation
limit: Ghalib's register depends on Rekhta/Urdu idiom that English versions flatten;
Rūpa Gosvāmī's Sanskrit terms (sakhya, visrambha, sthāyi-bhāva) do not map cleanly
onto English "warmth."

**Japanese (load-bearing).** Bashō's *Oku no Hosomichi* (1689 journey; haibun of
prose and haiku) records a long walk taken with a companion, Sora, and addressed to
readers as fellow travellers; the narrator's humility and attention to ordinary
places produce a companionable, unhurried warmth. Translation limit: the haibun's
rhythm and seasonal allusion depend on Japanese prosody and kigo conventions that
English translations approximate rather than reproduce.

**Persian (load-bearing).** Sa'di's *Gulistan* (1258) offers moral instruction in
an adab register described as simple, elegant and hospitable; its own framing
presents severe counsel as medicine mixed with the "honey of wit." Translation
limit: the Persian wordplay and the *Gulistan*'s alternating prose/verse structure
are only partially recoverable.

**Southern African (secondary but named).** Ramose's *African Philosophy Through
Ubuntu* (1999) articulates ubuntu humaneness through the maxim *umuntu ngumuntu
ngabantu* — a person is a person through other persons — grounding affiliation in
ontology rather than manners. This is a conceptual anchor for why affiliative
address matters, not a claim about technique.

**Anti-false-universal note.** Brown and Levinson claimed universality for their
face model, but cross-cultural pragmatics has repeatedly contested the
transferability of "positive politeness" across languages (e.g., Japanese
solidarity and honorific systems behave differently). The dossier therefore treats
the *function* of warmth — signalling friendly intent — as broadly present, while
treating the *forms* as culture-specific.

## G. Failure and misuse research

**Patronizing chumminess (over-accommodation).** CAT's over-accommodation is the
precise mechanism: adapting style beyond the audience's level reads as
condescension. Warmth that simplifies the reader (baby-talk, over-explanation,
"buddy" epithets) collapses into the very distance it pretends to remove
(`clm-tone-warm-convergence-overaccommodation`).

**Forced friendliness and surface acting (Hochschild 1983).** Hochschild's study
of flight attendants documents emotional labour: workers trained to display
friendliness they do not feel, producing surface acting and self-estrangement.
Passengers, she notes, are "quick to detect strained or forced smiles." A brand
voice that performs warmth it does not earn is read as exactly this
(`clm-tone-warm-warmth-without-substance`).

**Brand-voice smarm and over-politeness.** Aaker (1997) identifies **sincerity** as
a distinct brand-personality dimension (down-to-earth, honest, wholesome,
cheerful) — warmth in commerce is a construct to be *earned by substance*, not
sprinkled on. Leech (2014) supplies the linguistic failure: overpoliteness can be
received as sarcasm, and conventional politeness loses meaning when it becomes
obligatory. Stock phrases ("Hey friends!", "we're so excited") are the text
equivalent of the strained smile.

**Warmth without competence.** The SCM is explicit: high warmth with low competence
draws pity, not admiration (Fiske et al. 2002). A warm voice over weak substance
invites the reader to condescend to the writer — the inversion of the tone's goal.

**Tonal collapse into other registers.** Over grief, warmth reads as invalidation
and the correct surface is `tone-compassionate`; over a real deadline it reads as
evasion and the surface is `tone-urgent`; before the sacred it reads as
over-familiarity and the surface is `tone-reverent`. Warmth must know what it is
not.

**Cultural mismatch.** Because warm forms are culture-specific (§E), a technique
that signals affiliation in one tradition can signal intrusion or over-familiarity
in another. The tone travels as a *function*, not as a fixed set of phrases.

## H. Dosage and saturation findings

1. **Warmth is strongest as a frame, not a continuous register.** Because warmth
   is judged first and colours later traits (Asch 1946; Fiske et al. 2007), a brief
   warm establishment at the opening and at transitions does most of the work. A
   continuous warm surface stops being informative.

2. **Every warm beat must carry substance.** High warmth/low competence maps to
   pity (Fiske et al. 2002). Practical rule: if a warm passage can have every
   named fact, person and example deleted without loss, it is smarm, not warmth.

3. **Direct address has diminishing returns.** Parasocial intensity depends on
   *addressing the audience*, but the effect is per-contact, not per-sentence
   (Horton & Wohl 1956; Hartmann & Goldhoorn 2011). Saturation signal: second
   person in nearly every sentence with no new subject.

4. **Over-politeness and obligation invert the signal.** When courtesy becomes
   obligatory or excessive it can read as sarcasm or insincerity (Leech 2014;
   Hochschild 1983). Saturation signal: stacked intensifiers, thanks, and
   endearments where nothing is actually being offered.

5. **Where the tone must yield.** Stakes peaks and direct address of suffering
   require an unadorned or `tone-compassionate` surface; genuine deadlines require
   `tone-urgent`; sacred subject matter requires `tone-reverent`. Warmth dropped
   deliberately and then restored reads as more sincere than warmth held flat.

6. **Saturation signals (observable).** (a) Three or more consecutive sentences of
   direct address with no new fact, image or example; (b) endearment terms
   ("friend", "buddy", "folks") outnumbering concrete nouns; (c) exclamation marks
   in clusters; (d) humour that targets the reader rather than a shared third
   thing; (e) the passage survives deletion of every named person and thing.
